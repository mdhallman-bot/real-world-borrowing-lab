2. Checkout Trap — Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL)

BNPL is a type of installment loan. A common version splits a purchase into four payments, often with little or no interest. The problem we are investigating is not “BNPL = bad.” It is whether several small obligations still fit the same budget.

3. Short-Term Credit Simulation — Why Would Someone Say Yes?

Scenario: Your car needs a $500 repair today. Without the repair, you may miss work. You have only $50 in savings and payday is still a week away.

Short-term credit can have a real benefit: speed and access to money for an urgent need. The risk is what repayment does to the next paycheck—especially if that paycheck was already committed to necessities.

Suppose you borrow again because the $425 shortage cannot be covered. The immediate benefit is that necessities can be paid. But another repayment obligation follows you into the next pay period.

Debt-cycle risk: If the underlying gap between income and expenses has not changed, new borrowing can solve today's shortage while reducing money available from the next paycheck.

Colorado reality check

Colorado voters approved a 36% APR cap on deferred-deposit/payday loans in 2018. A Colorado Attorney General study reports that traditional deferred-deposit lenders then stopped making those loans in Colorado and some moved to other small-dollar products.

The Colorado Attorney General also filed a lawsuit against EarnIn in August 2026 involving its direct-to-consumer “Cash Out” product. The state alleges that tips and expedited-transfer charges functioned as finance charges and that the product violated Colorado lending laws. Those are allegations in an ongoing case, not a final finding.

4. Real Fine Print — What Does the Headline Leave Out?

This is based on a real example published in Upgrade's personal-loan disclosure. Instead of telling you what matters, your job is to pull the important information out of the disclosure.

REAL PUBLISHED PERSONAL-LOAN EXAMPLE • UPGRADE

Headline: $10,000 personal loan

Published example: 36-month term • 17.59% APR • 13.94% yearly interest rate • 5% one-time origination fee.

The fee is deducted from the loan proceeds, so the borrower receives $9,500. Upgrade's published example lists a required payment of $341.48/month and total payments of $12,293.46.

This is a lender-published example, not a personalized offer to you. Actual rates/terms depend on eligibility and other factors. Upgrade currently discloses personal-loan APRs of 7.74%–35.99%, origination fees of 1.85%–9.99%, and terms from 24–84 months.
